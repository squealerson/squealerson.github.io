Our family moved a lot when I was young. It was a good thing though as my father was offered promotions to transfer to new cities and help grow the retail business he worked for in Minnesota. I hardly remember either North Dakota or Montana as we lived there only a short time. Colorado felt more like home. I was only in first grade when we arrived in the small suburb of Denver, but I have some very clear core memories of that place. Playing baseball, football, and basketball in the backyard. My friends from little league and school. The neighbor girls who we played with frequently when my parents hung out with their parents playing cards. We were there for almost eight years and when you're only twelve or thirteen years old that seems like a lifetime. I felt like I belonged there having finally started to develop as a person. It was home to us and we, my younger brother and older sister, planned to stay for a long time.

Until we found out that dad was being transferred again. It was a good opportunity for him. A bigger territory, more responsibility, more money, company car, and stock incentives. It was an opportunity to do better for the family. He couldn't pass it up. We were a strong family and had moved before so it made sense. Just one more move and we could pick most anyplace within his new territory. Idaho was fairly centralized for the area and my parents evaluated several of the cities in the state. They finally decided on Idaho Falls. It was central to my father's territory, was a small, safe community, and seemed clean and friendly.

My sister was furious and completely against it. She was just finishing her junior year of high school and wanted to graduate the next year with her class and her friends. She tried to convince my parents that she could stay behind to live with a friend and join the rest of the family once high school was done. While it was discussed as a family, it was decided that she would have to join the rest of us to our new home. There would be no breaking up the family over this relocation. 

My brother felt it was important to support dad but he was young. Still in elementary school, keeping the family together seemed most important to him. He could be happy living anywhere as long as that didn't change.

I wasn't really sure how to feel. Of course I wanted to support my father and I knew he wanted to take the new job. He had already passed up an opportunity several years earlier that would have taken us all to Canada. But I had finally built some friendships as the constant moving made that difficult for me. I was having fun playing baseball that summer. One of the coaches had told my father that he would draft me into the higher league right away if we were staying. I was torn but mostly kept my feelings to myself as there was already plenty of drama with my sister. I didn't want to add more stress to the situation. Besides, we had moved before and I had started over. My brother and I had studied the almanac to learn as much as we could about the new place. It would be okay I told myself. A fresh start would be fine and a new adventure. 

So before school started my parents, brother, and I took off for Idaho Falls to look for houses. My sister stayed behind with her friend still hoping that she wouldn't be forced to leave her high school behind. We stayed in a hotel for a week sometimes getting to go to different houses with my parents. Mostly my brother and I were just hanging out at the hotel pool and running wild in the hallways of the near empty hotel while my parents would look for a place to live. We had a lot of fun running around unsupervised and we both decided that it was going to be a great place to live.

The process took longer than expected as a suitable and affordable house wasn't easy to find. School was to start soon so we returned to Colorado without a place to live yet. We were told not to worry that they would get us a good place to live. We would be moved in time for the start of school or at least close to the beginning of the school year. But as the move continued to be delayed, we had to start school in Colorado. My sister started her senior year of high school, I started as a ninth grader, and my brother started as a sixth grader. We were all in different schools and we all had our own challenges starting a new school year knowing that we would be moving in a few short weeks.

I didn't see the point of making an effort in school at that point. The teachers all knew I was leaving so missed assignments were ignored. I mostly just goofed off only half paying attention during class. None of it mattered, I thought, as I'd have new classes when I arrived in Idaho. None of it did matter but it made me feel a bit lost to be attending school without any intention of finishing or trying to learn anything new.

My parents had finally found a place on the west side of our new town. A split level with four bedrooms and two bathrooms. It was really three bedrooms with another in the basement that was yet to be finished. The basement bedroom was to be shared by my brother and me. My parents would have the big master bedroom, my sister would get the other big bedroom upstairs, and my grandmother would get the smaller upstairs bedroom. I was disappointed that I still wouldn't be getting my own room. My maternal grandmother had been living with us off and on in Colorado and had her own room there too so it just seemed normal that she would have a room in the new place. That's my mother's story though so I won't dwell on the fact that her living with us meant that I would be sharing a room with my brother.

As we went down to the unfinished bedroom in the basement, we were greeting with a cement floor painted blue with bright pink painted letters stating it as "The Pink Pit". We thought it was funny and my dad reassured us that he would fix it up by paneling the concrete walls and laying down carpet. It was the first thing he did in the new house and we had our own bedroom finished by the first weekend there.

But the new house seemed cramped to me. The big family room we had in Colorado was exchanged for a big living room in Idaho where my parents could entertain friends. There was no place to put the ping pong table that we played on for hours in our old basement. Or a second TV where we could watch what we wanted. The big bathroom upstairs was to be used by my sister, grandmother, and parents. The tiny bathroom that was in the family room would be for me and my brother. Since the family room was the only room with a TV, using the bathroom meant that everyone watching TV would be right outside the door of the tiny room. I hated it immediately as I suddenly had a lot less privacy than I did in the old house. I desperately wanted my own room and my own space but it quickly became clear that it wasn't going to happen. As a fourteen year old prepubescent boy, I was trapped in this place for the foreseeable future. It had happened so fast I just accepted it and kept my feelings to myself.

The next week we were each taken to our respective schools to enroll for the school year. School had already started about two or three weeks prior but I was assured that it wouldn't affect my standing with the teachers or other students. My mother and I met with my school counselor to sign me up for classes. I was a bit taken aback by this process because my schedule at my old school was very straightforward. Something like Math, English, Social Studies, Science, and an elective such as shop class or home economics. It was very simple there. At the new school, I was asked what classes I wanted and it was then that I discovered the science class was full. I would need to find something else, but most of the classes were full. Finally the counselor told me that I would need to enroll in a language class as that was really the only thing available. Did I want German or Spanish? I picked German because I knew that my uncle spoke German fluently as he was there during World War II. Then came the question about sixth period. It had to be either competitive sports or study hall. I had always played sports and wanted to try out for the football team. He told me that was good and I would enroll in the competitive sports class then tell the coaches I wanted play. 

When I arrived at the locker room for competitive sports the next day, all the players were donning their equipment so I expected the coach would help me pick out my helmet and pads. Instead, I was told that I was too late to play on the football team as there was no room for new players now. Everyone had already gone through training camp in preparation for the season so I would be too far behind the other players. Instead I was told I would be joining the cross country team as he tossed me some shorts and a sweatshirt. I was stunned because I hated running and competing in such an activity was the last thing I wanted to do. Nonetheless I was directed to another room then told to start running around town with the other cross country runners. I wanted to cry.

The cross country season was only a couple of months long because it would get too cold and snowy to be outside running. I performed poorly as a long distance runner and I was eager leave cross country behind once basketball started. There were a lot of kids wanting to play basketball, but there was only room for fifteen players. I thought I played well during tryouts but ended up being the last player cut. The coach said he didn't like how I shot free throws and that was the reason I wouldn't make the team. I knew I wasn't a bad free throw shooter but that was the decision as he didn't like my form. I cried in the backseat of my mother's car as she drove me home after that last practice. I didn't know what I would be doing now and wished I signed up for study hall. The next day I learned that missing the cut for basketball meant I would be joining the wrestling team. It was another sport I had no interest in playing and I hated that too. Fortunately I never had to actually compete as those kids were way too intense for my liking but I suffered through wrestling practice everyday.

Nobody realized it at the time, including myself, but I had become quite depressed about my circumstance. I had few friends, no sports to play, and nothing to do in my free time. My after school time became lying on the couch watching reruns on TV and playing through chess books on the small coffee table in the family room. Needless to say I was miserable. There didn't seem to be any other option for me at the time so I just went through the motions going to class, then to wrestling practice, then home to mope about.

My school work suffered. The classes and expectations were different than what I had experienced before. I was penalized for not making up the work from the first few weeks I missed even though I was told that wouldn't be the case. My father was traveling for work a lot of the time now and I felt isolated. Most of the kids and teachers belonged with the same religious organization. They excluded anyone that didn't belong to their tribe and that included me. I later discovered that the reason the science class was full was because it was all the Mormon kids getting an easy grade from the teacher who was a bishop in the church. The class wasn't full for other Mormon kids just for me. I wasn't a standout basketball player and so I got cut in favor of another Mormon kid. I was the best baseball player on the field when I tried out that next summer, but another Mormon kid was picked instead of me. Even now, I still feel bitter to have been excluded from so many activities just because I didn't attend the same church. 

The next year I entered the big high school and my sister went off to college. I suggested that either my brother or myself move into her old room so that we could both have our own room. That idea was dismissed because she would still need a room when she came home over the summer. So her bedroom remained empty while my brother and I stayed in basement. Later when I went off to college, my brother moved into the big bedroom that had been my sister's room and my sister's stuff was moved to the basement. When I came home for the summer to discover that my room was now the shared bedroom upstairs I wanted to scream how unfair it was. I felt like I no longer had a space at all in that house. 

I eventually found my friends though. They were the ones that skipped school and hung out at houses without supervision to drink and smoke pot. Every time someone's parents were out of town there would be a party. I stayed out late and got into trouble. I don't mean to blame anyone as I have learned that nobody is responsible for me other than myself. I often wonder, however, how things might have been different if we had never moved to that place and into that house.
