I graduated from college a few months before my 25th birthday. At least that's what I told my friends and family. I got the cap and gown, sat with my classmates, and walked across the stage as my name was called to receive my diploma. Of course they don't really hand out actual diplomas that way, it was just the hardboard cover to hold the diploma. The actual diploma would be sent once it was verified that all requirements had been met. I knew that I hadn't met all the requirements. I was a few credits short and had failed most of my classes that final semester. It was the second time I had flunked out, the first being a year prior. But it had been seven years since I had started college and it was expected that I would be ready to move into the work force and begin a real career. There would be no diploma coming in the mail for me but I couldn't face my parents to tell them I had failed again. So I just pretended to graduate not really knowing what I would do afterward.

I had my first drink at 15 during a high school football game. I was the new kid at school, having moved to town only about a year earlier. My new friends and I snuck cans of Miller High Life beer into the game in our big puffy jackets. We sat high in the stands toward one end and drank them. Not only did I like how they made me feel, I surprisingly liked the taste too. The bitterness of the cold beer in the cool November air was very appealing to me. It was also part of the culture then too. We partied every weekend in high school and sometimes during the school day. None of us were of legal age, but beer, wine, and liquor, were readily available if you tried hard enough to find it.

When I finally got to college a few years later, it felt like I had been set free. No longer under the purview of my parents, daily drinking started to become the norm. I had coasted through high school without having to really study at all and college freshman classes were very much the same. I would go to class, study the material for a few hours before the exam, then finish with an A or at worst a B. The rest of time we would party. The legal drinking age at the time was only 19, so it wasn't long before I could start frequenting the local bars that catered to the college crowd. While it seemed everyone smoked pot, we would also occasionally find other drugs to try - mushrooms, acid, maybe some random pills. But beer was everywhere. Keggers at some frat house, quarter beer night at the sandwich shop just off campus, or just a 12 pack of cans that I would keep cold in my small dorm fridge. I earned the nickname "Ralph" after throwing up all over the table while losing at some drinking game. College became a full time party for me.

By my third year, things were going downhill. I had joined the debate team as I had some experience and a few friends that were participating in tournaments across the west. We had stopped in Reno on the way back from a tournament in northern California to deal with a minor car issue. With nothing to do for a couple of hours, a few of us went to a casino to play some blackjack. I got lucky and won a little bit of money. So I had the graduate advisor drop us at the store to pick up some supplies for the long drive home. I picked up a case of beer and brought it onto the van with the 10 or so other students. We spent the next few hours drinking it all during the drive. Needless to say, the debate coach discovered we were drinking on the van, a clear violation of both school and state policy. Everyone on the van was called into a one on one meeting with the coach and asked if they had been drinking on the van. One by one they were suspended from the team. When it was my turn to meet with the coach, I didn't even have the courage to show up. I just quit instead of facing up to my transgression. That ended the debate program at our school as there weren't enough participants to continue. I felt bad about it, but not bad enough to make any changes in my behavior.

Our school had a program office that coordinated various campus events such as movies, speakers, small musical performances, and dances. I got appointed as the dance committee chairman responsible for a budget to plan and coordinate various dances on campus. The first event I hosted was fine as it was held at a local bar where I hired a band comprised of other students. Everyone drank a lot and it was fun. Every other event was a disaster. One of the biggest events was supposed to be the Military Ball held on Valentine's Day. I waited too long to do anything in preparation, then hired a DJ at the last minute to do the music for the event. They had specifically asked for a live band, but there was no chance of finding one with the event only two weeks away. I didn't even bother to show up to the event even though I was responsible for its success. By word of mouth I heard that everyone was very disappointed and that the event was a disaster. I was told that I would no longer be the dance committee chairperson (committee of one since I never asked for any help) and lost the small scholarship I was given for the role.

I stopped going to classes and by my fourth year I failed a class for the first time. A lot of that time is hazy in my memory but one incident stands out. I had a marketing class and I went to the class twice, both during the first week of the semester. The professor was a boisterous man and while I didn't really like him, he was fairly popular because his class was pretty easy and he kept it light. He had said that anyone could take the average of all their exam grades or take the results of the final exam as their final grade. Since I had never taken any of the exams, I showed up for the final, ready to accept what ever grade I could manage. As I sat in the unfamiliar classroom before the exam started the professor walked through the aisle looking at everyone that was there for the test. As he got to me he stopped and said "I think you're in the wrong classroom". I told him that I was there for the marketing exam. He looked at me incredulously and said "Marketing 325?". I told him that yes, that was the class. He laughed loudly and looked around the classroom then exclaimed "I've never seen you before in my life, hahahaha, good luck, you'll need it". I was mortified to be singled out like that but I obviously deserved it. I took the exam and finished the class with a D. I felt vindicated as I didn't fail, but it really didn't matter at that point.

The next year I managed to get straight Fs. I was unable to pass a single class. I was put on academic probation and dropped out of school. I went home to my parents house and found a job working the weekend graveyard shift at a local truck stop. It was an easy job and I could sometimes drink while working as there wasn't anything going on. I'm pretty sure the owner knew as he would relieve me at 6AM. But he never said anything as the till was accurate, the work was done, and he didn't want to try to find someone else willing to give up their weekends working graveyard shift.

I worked at the truck stop through the next semester and then the summer. My parents were adamant that it was unacceptable to give up on college and work a minimum wage job. I had to go back to school and finish my degree. So I re-enrolled for the fall semester, signing up for most of the classes I needed to graduate. I found a place to stay near campus in a house with five other roommates. Money was tight so I got a part time afternoon job delivering overnight freight for a small regional company. The money was better than most other jobs in the area so I felt like I was doing great. The first semester turned turned out pretty good as I earned some credits toward my degree and I could see the finish line. With only a few more core requirements to fulfill I thought I could get it done. But the freight company wasn't the greatest environment. Several of the drivers were using cocaine and speed regularly so I would occasionally find myself trying those drugs too. Some of my roommates were heavily into coke so it was readily available. The drug and alcohol abuse was catching up with me again.

That final semester turned into a disaster. I failed all but one of my classes and did not have the credits to graduate. I couldn't admit to anyone that I wasn't going to graduate with any of the people in my class. So I faked it. I walked across the stage when my name was called, my friends and family cheering me on. There were no graduation parties or celebrations for me. I was miserable but knew I had to continue the charade and go out into the real world to start my now promising career.
